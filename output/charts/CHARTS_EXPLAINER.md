# Charts — one paragraph each

Every chart in this folder, what it shows, and what to actually conclude from
it. Charts 01–03 cover the verbalized half of the project (what models *say*),
07 the internal-vs-verbalized coupling, 09–10 the causal steering test, and
11–14 the internal probe itself: both readout points, and the null models that
say which of its results survive.

Generated by `python -m evalaware.cli plots`. All 14 regenerate from the data
currently on disk, so nothing here is stale.

Numbering is not contiguous. 04, 05, 06 and 08 were deleted rather than
renumbered, so that older drafts referring to "chart 06" resolve to something
that no longer exists instead of silently resolving to a different chart. The
reasons are at the bottom.

---

## 01_ensemble_auroc_bar.png

The headline verbalized result: for each of the nine models we benchmarked, how
well its *stated* answer separates evaluation transcripts from deployment ones.
Each model saw all 20 transcripts and was asked, in five different phrasings,
whether the interaction was an evaluation; the five binary answers are averaged
per transcript and scored as AUROC against ground truth. The range is 1.000
(Qwen3-8B, GPT-5.6-terra, Qwen3.6-27B-OBLITERATED) down to 0.830
(Llama-3.1-8B), so **no model is at chance**. Three of these bars moved
substantially after a parsing bug in `resolve_answer()` was fixed; the
Qwen3.6-27B-OBLITERATED bar went from below chance to 1.000, which retracted
the project's former headline finding.

## 02_probe_heatmap.png

The same verbalized AUROC broken out by probe question rather than averaged:
models on one axis, the five phrasings (`direct_a`, `direct_vn_a`,
`h_purpose_analysis_a`, `h_user_or_control_set_a`, `h_intent_and_style_a`) on
the other. This exists to answer "did we just get lucky with one wording?" The
answer is largely no: strong models are strong across the row. Where a single
cell is much darker than its neighbours, that phrasing is doing something
different, and it is worth reading the raw responses in
`output/api/<model>/verbalized_responses.csv` before trusting that cell.

## 03_probe_lines.png

The same matrix as one line per model across the five phrasings, which makes
*stability under rewording* easier to see than a colour grid does. A flat line
means the model's judgement is robust to how you ask; a jagged line means the
measurement is partly measuring the question rather than the model. Use this to
decide whether the 5-probe ensemble in chart 01 is averaging away real noise or
hiding a real disagreement.

## 07_coupling_layers_last_rho.png

Experiment 2, last-token readout: does the internal probe score move together
with what the model *says*? For every layer, each transcript's probe score is
rank-correlated (|Spearman ρ|) against the mean of its five verbalized answers,
with the random-direction band drawn underneath. Peaks are 0.837 @L34
(Qwen3.6-27B-OBLITERATED), 0.639 @L29 (Qwen3-8B) and 0.620 @L32
(granite-4.2-30b), far above the near-zero coupling (|ρ| < 0.19) the source
paper reports. **This is almost certainly not a discovery.** Both quantities
are decent classifiers of the same ground-truth label, so they correlate
whether or not one causes the other; because we ask a direct yes/no question
rather than grading spontaneous mentions, our verbalized side is a much better
label-classifier than the paper's, which mechanically inflates ρ. Note also
that Qwen3-8B's peak sits *below* its own random band at that layer (0.639 vs
0.867), which is visible in the chart and is why the peak-summary version was
deleted.

## 07_coupling_layers_last_mi.png

The same pairing measured with Kraskov k-nearest-neighbour mutual information
instead of rank correlation, in nats. MI is included because it catches
*non-monotonic* dependence that Spearman misses entirely: a relationship where
the internal score is high at both extremes would give ρ ≈ 0 but MI > 0. Peaks
are 0.479 (Qwen3-8B, L18), 0.474 (Qwen3.6-27B-OBLITERATED, L16) and 0.379
(granite-4.2-30b, L10). The same shared-ground-truth confound applies here, so
read the two together rather than as independent confirmations.

## 07_coupling_layers_mean_rho.png

The ρ coupling chart under mean-token pooling. Its main use is as a robustness
check on the last-token version: if a model's coupling peak appears under one
pooling and vanishes under the other, that peak is a property of the readout
choice rather than of the model, and should not be quoted.

## 07_coupling_layers_mean_mi.png

The mutual-information coupling chart under mean-token pooling, completing the
2×2 of {ρ, MI} × {last, mean}. One implementation caveat worth knowing:
`evalaware/metrics.py` estimates MI with `mutual_info_regression`,
which treats the target as continuous. That is defensible for the 5-probe
ensemble (six distinct levels) but wrong for the individual binary probes,
where `mutual_info_classif`, what the reference implementation uses, is
correct.

**Not plotted here:** the prompt-only coupling. Recomputing all of the above
from the prompt-only activations severs the shared-answer pathway, and coupling
barely moves (0.639 → 0.676, 0.837 → 0.804, 0.620 → 0.817 on the last-token
readout). That result lives in the report as `tab:coupling-readouts` rather
than as a chart.

## 09_steering_effect.png

Experiment 3, the causal test: instead of observing the internal direction, we
*inject* it during generation and watch whether the model's stated answer
moves. At a chosen layer, `α · c_L` is added to the residual stream at every
token position, where `c_L` is the probe direction scaled to that layer's
typical activation magnitude. The chart plots the change in mean `eval_signal`
against the unsteered baseline, per layer, one line per steering strength α.
All three models were re-run after a context bug and now share a baseline of
0.500. Steering moves the answer, by up to +0.500 on a 0–1 scale, **but it
moves it upward at both signs of α**: 34 of the 44 scoreable cells go up, 4
down, 6 unchanged. That contradicts the sign convention the experiment was
built around.

## 10_steering_peak_summary.png

The largest steering effect per model, split by direction of α. Check
`n_scored` in `output/local/<model>/results/steering_results.json` before
reading much into it: at α = ±1.0 the injection is comparable to the layer's
entire activation norm and many cells lose transcripts to unparseable output
(7/16 cells for Qwen3-8B, 8/16 for Qwen3.6-27B-OBLITERATED, 0/16 for granite).
Those dropped generations are not one thing. With the raw text now retained we
can see that Qwen3-8B's 31 failures are 28 degenerate repetition loops, but two
of Qwen3.6's are the model coherently answering the transcript's *own*
multiple-choice question (`<label>C</label>`, `<label>3</label>`) instead of
ours. Strong steering induces task confusion as well as degeneration, and that
gets scored as missing data rather than as an effect. There is also still no
random-direction steering arm, so we cannot rule out that any vector of this
magnitude would shift the answer just as much.

## 11_prompt_vs_answer_layers_last.png

Experiment 1b, last-token readout: the same probe direction read at two points,
per layer. The dashed pale line is the original readout (activations pooled
over prompt **plus the model's own generated answer**); the solid line is the
prompt-only readout, taken before a single answer token exists. This matters
because the old readout's last token is the last token the model *wrote*, and
for an MMLU transcript that is often the end of a LaTeX-boxed answer letter, so
the probe was partly reading the model's own output format back to itself.
Qwen3-8B drops 0.980 → 0.870 and its prompt-only peak lands at **layer 1 of
36**, which is surface lexical territory. The dotted orange line is the 95th
percentile of the label-permutation null.

## 11_prompt_vs_answer_layers_mean.png

The same comparison under mean-token pooling, and the more informative of the
two. Averaging over the prompt's content tokens is where the prompt-only signal
actually lives: Qwen3-8B goes 0.540 → 0.920, Qwen3.6-27B-OBLITERATED
0.700 → 0.890, granite-4.2-30b 0.770 → 0.810. There is a mechanical reason for
the last/mean split: every prompt ends in the same fixed chat-template suffix
(`<|im_start|>assistant\n`, or `…<think>\n` for the thinking models), identical
across all 20 transcripts, so a last-token readout over the prompt is looking
at a position whose content does not vary with the label.

## 12_prompt_vs_answer_peaks.png

Peak AUROC for both readouts and both poolings side by side, with each model's
label-permutation p95 marked. The pattern worth noticing is that the two
readouts do not rank the same way: the best prompt+answer result (Qwen3-8B
last-token) and the best prompt-only result (Qwen3-8B mean-token) come from
opposite pooling choices. A gap of 0.06 between two bars here is well inside
the noise at n=20, so the bars are only meaningful relative to the permutation
line, not to each other.

## 13_probe_null_controls.png

Every internal-probe result (3 models × 2 readouts × 2 poolings) plotted
against the null models that actually apply, from
`evalaware/metrics.py`. The bar is the reported peak AUROC; the
orange tick is the 95th percentile of a label-permutation null (same probe,
same activations, same `argmax`, shuffled labels); the grey square is the mean
of ten random directions each given *its own* best layer; the triangle is `‖h‖`
alone with no probe direction; the black diamond is the probe score with
magnitude divided out; and the dashed vertical line at 0.83 is what **prompt
token count alone** achieves. Under the `argmax` statistic shown here, only 4
of 12 cells clear the permutation null at p<0.05, none clears the
random-direction null, and seven fail to beat prompt length. **Read this
together with chart 14** — on its own it tells only the pessimistic half of the
story.

## 14_band_statistic.png

**The chart that says what actually survived.** Chart 13's universal failure
turned out to be caused by the *statistic*, not the data: at n=20 a random
direction's per-layer AUROC has `|AUROC − 0.5|` of 0.16–0.23, and adjacent
layers are near-independent (measured correlation 0.00–0.032), so `argmax` over
36–64 layers is ~40 independent lottery tickets and reaches ≈0.9 under the
null. This chart replaces it with a **3-layer band** statistic (a real
representation should persist across neighbouring layers; a random direction
has no reason to) and re-tests against a label-permutation null and **1000
fresh random directions** rather than the 10 stored in `probe.pt`, which floors
any p-value at 1/11. Solid bar is the band statistic, faded bar the old
`argmax`, grey square the random-direction mean, orange tick the permutation
p95. Three of twelve cells clear both nulls: **Qwen3-8B prompt+answer/last**
(band 0.930 @L19–21, perm p=0.0008, random p=0.0060), **Qwen3-8B
prompt-only/mean** (0.910 @L31–33, p=0.0008 / 0.0030) and
**Qwen3.6-27B-OBLITERATED prompt-only/mean** (0.853 @L19–21, p=0.0240 /
0.0160); the first two survive Bonferroni across all twelve. Two of the three
are prompt-only, so the signal does exist before generation. **Caveat to state
whenever this chart is used:** the band statistic was adopted *after* the
`argmax` analysis returned null, making it a post-hoc choice. It is applied
uniformly to all twelve cells with identical nulls, but it is a forking path
and must be declared as one.

## Deleted charts, and why

**`04_probe_grouped_bar.png`** — the same 9×5 verbalized-AUROC matrix as charts
02 and 03, grouped by probe instead of by model. A transpose of chart 03
carrying no information the other two do not already show. Its generator
`plot_probe_grouped_bar()` was removed with it.

**`05_internal_auroc_layers_{last,mean}.png` and
`06_internal_auroc_peak_summary.png`** — these plotted the Experiment 1 peak
AUROC against nothing but the per-layer random-direction band, the comparison
chart 13 shows to be mis-specified: it lets the real probe pick its best of
36–64 layers while the control cannot. Anyone reading only those charts came
away with the inflated version of the result. Everything they showed is carried
by charts 11–14, which include the same per-layer curves and the correct nulls.
Their generator was removed with them.

**`08_coupling_peak_summary.png`** — the same defect as 06, one experiment
over. It reported a peak taken as `argmax` over 36–64 layers against a random
band computed at that same layer, and Qwen3-8B's bar (0.639) is actually
*below* its own random band at its peak layer (0.867). Presenting a value that
loses to noise as a headline bar is exactly what got 06 deleted. The per-layer
`07_*` charts stay, because they draw the band across every layer and let a
reader see the overlap for themselves. Its generator `plot_peak_summary()` was
removed with it.
