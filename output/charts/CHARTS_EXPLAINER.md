# Charts — one paragraph each

Every chart in this folder, what it shows, and what to actually conclude from
it. Charts 01–04 cover the verbalized half of the project (what models *say*),
05–08 the internal half (what their activations encode), 09–10 the causal test,
and 11–13 the prompt-only readout and the null models.

Generated by `analysis/plot_results.py` (01–04),
`analysis/plot_internal_results.py` (05–06), `analysis/plot_coupling.py`
(07–08), `analysis/plot_steering.py` (09–10) and
`analysis/plot_prompt_vs_answer.py` (11–13).

---

## 01_ensemble_auroc_bar.png

The headline verbalized result: for each of the nine models we benchmarked, how
well its *stated* answer separates evaluation transcripts from deployment ones.
Each model was shown all 20 transcripts and asked, in five different phrasings,
whether the interaction was an evaluation; the five binary answers are averaged
per transcript and scored as AUROC against ground truth. The range is 1.000
(Qwen3-8B, GPT-5.6-terra, Qwen3.6-27B-OBLITERATED) down to 0.830
(Llama-3.1-8B) — **no model is at chance**. Note that three of these bars moved
substantially after a parsing bug in `resolve_answer()` was fixed; the
Qwen3.6-27B-OBLITERATED bar in particular went from below chance to 1.000, which
retracted the project's former headline finding. See `explainer_docs/PROGRESS.md`
§10.

## 02_probe_heatmap.png

The same verbalized AUROC broken out by probe question rather than averaged —
models on one axis, the five phrasings (`direct_a`, `direct_vn_a`,
`h_purpose_analysis_a`, `h_user_or_control_set_a`, `h_intent_and_style_a`) on
the other. This exists to answer "did we just get lucky with one wording?" The
answer is largely no: strong models are strong across the row. Where a single
cell is much darker than its neighbours, that phrasing is doing something
different — worth reading the raw responses in
`output/api/<model>/verbalized_responses.csv` before trusting that cell.

## 03_probe_lines.png

The heatmap's information as one line per model across the five phrasings,
which makes *stability under rewording* easier to see than a colour grid does. A
flat line means the model's judgement is robust to how you ask; a jagged line
means the measurement is partly measuring the question, not the model. Use this
chart to decide whether the 5-probe ensemble in chart 01 is averaging away real
noise or hiding a real disagreement.

## 04_probe_grouped_bar.png

The transpose of chart 03 — grouped by probe question, one bar per model within
each group. This asks which *phrasing* is the most discriminating overall,
rather than which model. It is the chart to look at if you want to cut the probe
set down from five questions to one or two: pick the groups where most models
score high, since a phrasing that is weak for everyone is measuring something
other than eval-awareness.

## 05_internal_auroc_layers_last.png

Experiment 1, last-token readout: how well a *linear direction in the model's
activations* separates eval from deploy transcripts, at every layer, for the
three models we have hidden states for. The direction is built from 16
hand-written contrastive sentence pairs (`data/contrastive_pairs_16.txt`) with
no training and no exposure to the 20 transcripts. The coloured line is the real
probe, the shaded band is the maximum over ten random unit directions at each
layer, and the circle marks the reported peak (Qwen3-8B 0.980 @L19,
Qwen3.6-27B-OBLITERATED 0.880 @L24, granite-4.2-30b 0.730 @L13). **Read the
shaded band carefully**: it swallows almost the entire curve, and chart 13 shows
why the peak is not as impressive as it looks.

## 05_internal_auroc_layers_mean.png

The same chart with mean-token pooling — the probe score averaged over all
tokens instead of read at the last one. It is here because the choice of readout
turns out to do a large share of the work: Qwen3-8B drops from 0.980 (last) to
0.540 (mean), essentially a coin flip, while granite-4.2-30b *improves* from
0.730 to 0.770 and Qwen3.6-27B-OBLITERATED peaks at layer 0, which is barely
past the embeddings and almost certainly surface lexical statistics rather than
an abstraction. Compare this side by side with the `_last` version before
quoting any single number.

## 06_internal_auroc_peak_summary.png

The peak-layer AUROC from charts 05, collapsed to one bar per model with both
poolings shown, plus a dotted reference at 0.7 (the level all six models in the
source paper clear at their best layer). It is the fastest way to see the
model-to-model ordering, but it is also the most misleading chart in the folder
taken alone, because a peak selected by `argmax` over 36–64 layers at n=20 is
inflated by the selection itself. Always pair it with chart 13.

## 07_coupling_layers_last_rho.png

Experiment 2, last-token readout: does the internal probe score move together
with what the model *says*? For every layer, the transcript's probe score is
rank-correlated (|Spearman ρ|) against the mean of its five verbalized answers,
with the random-direction band drawn underneath. Peaks are 0.837 @L34
(Qwen3.6-27B-OBLITERATED), 0.639 @L29 (Qwen3-8B) and 0.620 @L32
(granite-4.2-30b) — far above the near-zero coupling (|ρ| < 0.19) the source
paper reports. **This is almost certainly not a discovery.** Both quantities are
decent classifiers of the same ground-truth label, so they correlate whether or
not one causes the other; and because we ask the model a direct yes/no question
rather than grading spontaneous mentions, our verbalized side is a much better
label-classifier than the paper's, which mechanically inflates ρ.

## 07_coupling_layers_last_mi.png

The same pairing measured with Kraskov k-nearest-neighbour mutual information
instead of rank correlation, in nats. MI is included because it catches
*non-monotonic* dependence that Spearman misses entirely — a relationship where
internal score is high at both extremes would give ρ ≈ 0 but MI > 0. Peaks are
0.479 (Qwen3-8B, L18), 0.474 (Qwen3.6-27B-OBLITERATED, L16) and 0.379
(granite-4.2-30b, L10). The same shared-ground-truth confound applies here as in
the ρ chart, so read them together, not as independent confirmations.

## 07_coupling_layers_mean_rho.png

The ρ coupling chart under mean-token pooling. Its main use is as a robustness
check on the last-token version: if a model's coupling peak appears under one
pooling and vanishes under the other, that peak is a property of the readout
choice rather than of the model, and should not be quoted.

## 07_coupling_layers_mean_mi.png

The mutual-information coupling chart under mean-token pooling, completing the
2×2 of {ρ, MI} × {last, mean}. One implementation caveat worth knowing:
`compare_internal_vs_verbal.py` estimates MI with `mutual_info_regression`,
which treats the target as continuous. That is defensible for the 5-probe
ensemble (six distinct levels) but wrong for the individual binary probes, where
`mutual_info_classif` — what the reference implementation uses — is correct.

## 08_coupling_peak_summary.png

The peak coupling values from the four 07 charts, collapsed into one comparison
per model. It is the chart that most directly contradicts the source paper, and
therefore the one most likely to be misread: the honest caption is "our
measurement design produces high coupling by construction", not "we found strong
coupling where the paper found none". If you want to actually test the paper's
claim, you need spontaneous verbalization scored by a judge, not elicited yes/no
answers — the reasoning traces needed for that are already on disk.

## 09_steering_effect.png

Experiment 3, the causal test: instead of observing the internal direction, we
*inject* it during generation and watch whether the model's stated answer moves.
At a chosen layer, `α · c_L` is added to the residual stream at every token
position, where `c_L` is the probe direction scaled to that layer's typical
activation magnitude. The chart plots the change in mean `eval_signal` versus
the unsteered baseline, per layer, one line per steering strength α. Steering
does move the answer, by up to +0.500 on a 0–1 scale, which is a large shift.
**But it moves it upward for both signs of α**, contradicting the sign
convention the experiment was designed around.

## 10_steering_peak_summary.png

The largest steering effect per model, split by direction of α. The number to
check before reading anything into this chart is `n_scored` in
`output/local/<model>/results/steering_results.json`: at α = ±1.0 the injection
is comparable to the layer's entire typical activation norm and the model stops
producing parseable output, so several cells are computed from one surviving
transcript or none at all. Only the α = ±0.5 cells with all six transcripts
scored are interpretable. There is also no random-direction steering arm, so we
cannot currently rule out that any vector of that magnitude would shift the
answer just as much.

## 11_prompt_vs_answer_layers_last.png

Experiment A, last-token readout: the same probe direction read at two different
points, per layer. The dashed pale line is the original readout (activations
pooled over prompt **plus the model's own generated answer**); the solid line is
the new prompt-only readout, taken before a single answer token exists. This
matters because the old readout's last token is the last token the model
*wrote* — for an MMLU transcript, literally the end of a LaTeX-boxed answer
letter — so the probe was partly reading the model's own output format back to
itself. Qwen3-8B drops 0.980 → 0.870 and its prompt-only peak lands at **layer
1 of 36**, which is surface lexical territory; the dotted orange line is the 95th
percentile of the label-permutation null.

## 11_prompt_vs_answer_layers_mean.png

The same before/after comparison under mean-token pooling, and this is the more
informative of the two. Averaging over the prompt's content tokens is where the
prompt-only signal actually lives: Qwen3-8B goes 0.540 (prompt+answer) → 0.920
(prompt-only), Qwen3.6-27B-OBLITERATED 0.700 → 0.890, granite-4.2-30b
0.770 → 0.810. There is a mechanical reason for the last/mean split: every
prompt ends in the same fixed chat-template suffix (`<|im_start|>assistant\n`,
or `…<think>\n` for the thinking models), identical across all 20 transcripts,
so there is little for a last-token readout to pick up.

## 12_prompt_vs_answer_peaks.png

Peak AUROC for both readouts and both poolings, side by side, with each model's
label-permutation p95 marked as a dotted line. The pattern worth noticing is
that the two readouts do not simply rank the same way: the best prompt+answer
result (Qwen3-8B last-token) and the best prompt-only result (Qwen3-8B
mean-token) come from opposite pooling choices. A delta of 0.06 between two bars
here is well inside the noise at n=20 — the bars are only meaningful relative to
the permutation line, not to each other.

## 13_probe_null_controls.png

**Read this together with chart 14 — on its own it tells only the pessimistic
half of the story.** Every internal-probe result
(3 models × 2 readouts × 2 poolings) plotted against the null models that
actually apply, from `analysis/probe_null_controls.py`. The bar is the reported
peak AUROC; the orange tick is the 95th percentile of a label-permutation null
(same probe, same activations, same `argmax`, shuffled labels); the grey square
is the mean of ten random directions each given *its own* best layer; the
triangle is `‖h‖` alone with no probe direction at all; the black diamond is the
probe score with magnitude divided out; and the dashed vertical line at 0.83 is
what **prompt token count alone** achieves. Under the `argmax` statistic shown
here, only 4 of 12 cells clear the permutation null at p<0.05, none clears the
random-direction null, and seven fail to beat prompt length. The diagnosis is
that `argmax` over 36–64 near-independent layers is itself the problem, which
chart 14 addresses — so read this chart as an indictment of the peak-selection
method as much as of the probe.

## 14_band_statistic.png

**The most important chart in the folder**, and the one that says what actually
survived. Chart 13's universal failure turned out to be caused by the
*statistic*, not the data: at n=20 a random direction's per-layer AUROC has
`|AUROC − 0.5|` of 0.16–0.23, and adjacent layers are near-independent
(measured correlation 0.00–0.032), so `argmax` over 36–64 layers is ~40
independent lottery tickets and reaches ≈0.9 under the null. This chart replaces
it with a **3-layer band** statistic — a real representation should persist
across neighbouring layers, a random direction has no reason to — and re-tests
against a label-permutation null and **1000 fresh random directions** rather
than the 10 stored in `probe.pt` (which floors any p-value at 1/11). Solid bar
is the band statistic, faded bar the old `argmax`, grey square the random-
direction mean, orange tick the permutation p95. Three of twelve cells clear
both nulls: **Qwen3-8B prompt+answer/last** (band 0.930 @L19–21, perm p=0.0008,
random p=0.0060), **Qwen3-8B prompt-only/mean** (0.910 @L31–33, p=0.0008 /
0.0030) and **Qwen3.6-27B-OBLITERATED prompt-only/mean** (0.853 @L19–21,
p=0.0240 / 0.0160); the first two survive Bonferroni across all twelve. Two of
the three are prompt-only, so the signal does exist before generation. **Caveat
to state whenever this chart is used:** the band statistic was adopted *after*
the `argmax` analysis returned null, making it a post-hoc choice — it is applied
uniformly to all twelve cells with identical nulls, but it is a forking path and
must be declared as one.
